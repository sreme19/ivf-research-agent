Where to publish, and on what

A briefing for Dr. Mekhala Iyengar, Indira IVF Rajaji Nagar, Bengaluru. Prepared November 3, 2026.
The short version
  1. Indira IVF colleagues produced 61 fertility items from 2023 to 2026. The largest topics are ovarian stimulation (14), luteal support and frozen transfer (11), and sperm DNA damage (10).
  2. 23 of the 61 are conference abstracts: 14 ESHRE posters, 6 congress abstracts in Fertility & Reproduction (the ASPIRE journal) and 3 ASRM abstracts. An abstract is the usual first step at Indira IVF.
  3. Most full papers are retrospective. They review the chain's own cycle records, often thousands of cycles. Randomized trials appear only from 2024.
  4. The Bangalore team, led by Dr. Shyam Gupta at JP Nagar, works on implantation failure, sperm retrieval and immune therapy for repeated miscarriage.
  5. Other Bangalore clinics publish mainly in one journal. The Journal of Human Reproductive Sciences (JHRS) carries 23 of their 69 papers since 2020. Apollo Fertility Bangalore has no indexed IVF papers.
  6. One gap stands out. No Indira IVF title mentions change in endometrial thickness.

6. How to write the abstract

ASPIRE format (inherited from 2024 to 2026, not yet confirmed for 2027):

  • Title of 25 words or fewer, in UPPER CASE.
  • Body of 300 words or fewer, including acknowledgements.
  • Four headings: Background and Aims, Methods, Results, Conclusions.
  • At most 3 tables and 2 images. At most 2 abstracts per author.

What reviewers look for:

  • One question and one main outcome, chosen before the data is seen.
  • Methods state design, center, period, number of cycles, inclusion rules and the statistical test.
  • Results give numbers with 95% confidence intervals. A p-value alone is not enough.
  • The conclusion says no more than the data shows. A null result is still publishable.

7. How to collect the data

  1. Get ethics committee approval first. For a record review, ask for a consent waiver. A case report needs the patient's written consent.
  2. Write the variable list before pulling any record, with the definition of each outcome. For example, state which scan and which finding count as clinical pregnancy.
  3. Record raw values, such as "8.7 mm" or "4AB." Never write "adequate" or "good." Make the categories later.
  4. Use a study ID only. No name, hospital number or phone number goes into the sheet. The ID key stays on the clinic's secured system.
  5. Use one row per cycle. Note when one woman has more than one cycle.
  6. Leave a cell blank when the value is unknown. Enter 0 only for "confirmed absent."
  7. Lock the analysis plan before looking at results. Do not choose a cutoff because it gives the best p-value.
