## Methods

### Study design

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT -->

We conducted a retrospective multicentre cohort study of women undergoing single
blastocyst frozen embryo transfer (FET). The study was designed and reported in
accordance with the STROBE statement for observational research.

The exposure of interest was the relative change in endometrial thickness between
progesterone initiation and the day of embryo transfer, expressed as an Endometrial
Stability Index (ESI). The primary outcome was implantation.

A statistical analysis plan specifying the primary exposure, outcome, adjustment
set, subgroup analyses and handling of missing data was finalised before any
analysis was undertaken. *[SAP lock date to be inserted — see PAPER-STATE Q1–Q4.]*

### Participants

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT -->

Women aged 21 to 45 years undergoing frozen transfer of a single blastocyst derived
from autologous oocytes were eligible.

Cycles were included where endometrial thickness had been measured at two
standardised time points — immediately before progesterone initiation and on the day
of embryo transfer — and where embryo grade, progesterone exposure duration and
transfer outcome were documented.

Cycles were excluded where any of the following applied: donor oocytes; gestational
carriage; major Müllerian anomaly; untreated hydrosalpinx; untreated significant
submucosal fibroid; significant untreated endometrial polyp; severe intrauterine
adhesions; absence of standardised endometrial measurement at either time point;
missing embryo information; or missing transfer outcome. Transfers of more than one
embryo were excluded from the primary analysis.

Adenomyosis, endometriosis and uterine fibroids not meeting the above exclusion
criteria were recorded rather than excluded, and were available for adjustment and
subgroup analysis.

transfer makes implantation per transfer and per embryo identical, which removes a

### Exposure: the Endometrial Stability Index

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT -->

Endometrial thickness was recorded at two time points. ET1 was measured immediately
before initiation of progesterone, preferably within 24 hours. ET2 was measured on
the day of embryo transfer, before the transfer procedure, using the same
methodology.

Absolute change was calculated as:

    ΔET (mm) = ET2 − ET1

The Endometrial Stability Index, the primary exposure, was calculated as the
relative change:

    ESI (%) = [(ET2 − ET1) / ET1] × 100

Negative values denote a reduction in thickness during progesterone exposure
(compaction), values near zero denote stability, and positive values denote
expansion. **These terms are descriptive. ESI was analysed as a continuous variable
and no categorical thresholds were imposed a priori.**

Both ΔET and ESI were derived computationally from the recorded ET1 and ET2 values;
neither was calculated manually at the point of data entry.

Because the duration of progesterone exposure before transfer may vary, an
exploratory rate variable was defined as ESI divided by the number of days of
progesterone exposure. This was not the primary exposure.

### Ultrasound methodology

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT — improves with site protocol documentation -->

Endometrial thickness was measured by transvaginal ultrasound in the sagittal
midline plane of the uterus, recording the maximum double-layer thickness with the
myometrium excluded, to the nearest 0.1 mm.

Endometrial pattern was recorded at both time points as triple-line, intermediate,
homogeneous/echogenic, or other/uncertain. Subjective descriptors were not used.

Date, time where available, operator and machine identifiers were recorded for each
measurement.

Because ESI is a difference between two measurements, it is sensitive to measurement
variation in a way that a single thickness reading is not. To quantify this, a random
sample of approximately 100 scans was independently re-measured by a second observer
blinded to the original value. Agreement for endometrial thickness was assessed by
intraclass correlation coefficient and agreement for endometrial pattern by Cohen's
kappa.

### Covariates

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT -->

Covariates were selected on clinical and causal grounds rather than by univariate
statistical screening, and comprised: maternal age at transfer; body mass index;
infertility diagnosis; number of previous failed transfers; baseline endometrial
thickness (ET1); endometrial pattern; endometrial preparation protocol; duration of
progesterone exposure; progesterone route and dose; serum progesterone before
transfer where available; duration of oestrogen exposure; blastocyst developmental
day; blastocyst expansion, inner cell mass and trophectoderm grades; and ploidy
status where preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy had been performed.
Participating centre was included to account for clustering.

Baseline endometrial thickness was retained in all models. Because ESI is defined
relative to ET1, omitting ET1 would leave the exposure confounded by starting
thickness by construction.

Collinearity among covariates was assessed. *[Method and result to follow.]*

### Limitations — partial draft

<!-- STATUS: DRAFT -->

Several limitations are already identifiable from the design and are recorded here
so that they are not discovered late:

**Endpoint.** Implantation is an early surrogate. The association between ESI and
live birth was not assessed, and a factor influencing implantation does not
necessarily influence the probability of a liveborn infant. Findings should not be
extrapolated to live birth.

**Retrospective design.** Endometrial measurements were obtained during routine care
rather than under a research protocol. The interval between ET1 and progesterone
initiation, and between ET2 and transfer, therefore varied, and this variation is
not fully captured by recorded date and time alone.

**Measurement variation in a multicentre setting.** ESI is a difference between two
measurements and is correspondingly more sensitive to inter-operator and
inter-machine variation than either measurement alone. The reproducibility substudy
quantifies but does not eliminate this.

**Ultrasound thickness is a surrogate phenotype.** It reflects endometrial structure
under progesterone exposure. It is not a direct measurement of endometrial
receptivity, and should not be interpreted as one.

**Residual confounding.** Despite adjustment for patient, cycle and embryo
characteristics, unmeasured factors — endometrial molecular status, undiagnosed
chronic endometritis, immunological factors — may influence both endometrial
behaviour and implantation.

**Generalisability.** The cohort derives from a single clinical network in India.
Practice patterns, patient characteristics and embryo culture conditions may differ
elsewhere.
