# Endometrial compaction and FET outcomes — what is already published

**Literature review for the Endometrial Stability Index (ESI) study**
Search date: **29 August 2026**. Companion data: [`evidence-table.md`](./evidence-table.md).

---

## Bottom line

The phenomenon the ESI study proposes to measure — percentage change in endometrial
thickness between progesterone start and transfer day — is one of the most intensively
studied ultrasound markers in frozen embryo transfer of the last seven years. It is known
as **endometrial compaction**, and there are roughly 25 primary cohort studies and **four
independent systematic reviews with meta-analysis** (2023–2024), pooling up to 27,857
patients. The consistent verdict of all four meta-analyses is the same: compaction is
associated with higher *clinical* and *ongoing* pregnancy rates in crude analyses of
hormone-replacement cycles, but **this does not survive to live birth** (pooled OR/RR
0.95–0.97, confidence intervals straddling 1 in every meta-analysis), and it **does not
hold in euploid-embryo cohorts** (pooled RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.86–1.13). Critically, the
single largest study ever performed — Pan 2025, 16,453 HRT-FET cycles — used the *exact*
ESI formula, `(EMT2−EMT1)/EMT1 × 100`, and found compaction associated with the *worst*
pregnancy rates. **The ESI study cannot be framed as investigating an unstudied
phenomenon.** Its defensible contribution is methodological, not one of priority.

## The landmark studies

**Haas J et al., *Fertility and Sterility* 2019 (n=274).** The origin paper — and note it
is *Fertility and Sterility*, **not** RBMO, which is a common mis-citation. In 274
HRT-prepared FET cycles the authors reported a "highly significant inverse correlation"
between ongoing pregnancy rate and change in endometrial thickness: the more the lining
compacted after progesterone, the better the outcome. The finding was striking enough to
launch the entire field. Its Achilles heel is that thickness was measured **transvaginally
before progesterone and transabdominally on transfer day** — two different acoustic
windows, which systematically under-reads the second measurement and manufactures apparent
"compaction".

**Zilberberg E et al. (with Haas and Casper), *Fertility and Sterility* 2020 (n=234).** The
same group's euploid follow-up, and the paper the ESI team most needs to be aware of: **234
elective single euploid (PGT-A) blastocyst transfers in hormonally prepared cycles**. With
≥15% compaction, ongoing pregnancy was 51.5% versus 30.2% without compaction. This is, in
population terms, essentially the ESI study's cohort — done six years ago, and positive.

**Riestenberg C et al., *JARG* 2021 (n=259) and Shah JS et al., *Human Reproduction* 2022
(n=186).** Two independent attempts to replicate Zilberberg in the same population, both
negative. Riestenberg studied 259 single euploid medicated FETs with **transvaginal
measurement at both timepoints** and found live birth rates of 58.1% (compaction), 54.7%
(no change) and 58.6% (expansion) — p=0.96. Shah's study is prospective, tested five
compaction thresholds from >0% to ≥20%, and found no prediction of live birth at any of
them, in either programmed or modified natural cycles. Shah's authors go further than
neutrality: they warn that assessing compaction "may prompt unnecessary cycle cancellation."

**Jin Z et al., *Reproductive Biology and Endocrinology* 2021 (n=219).** The most
uncomfortable paper for the compaction hypothesis, because it is the closest methodological
match to what ESI proposes. Single frozen-thawed **euploid** blastocyst transfers, thickness
change modelled **continuously as a ratio**, adjusted in multiple logistic regression. The
association was real and statistically significant — **in the opposite direction**: adjusted
OR 1.29 (95% CI 1.01–1.64) per unit *increase* in the change ratio. Non-compaction beat
compaction. Bu Z et al. (RBE 2019, n=3,091 single frozen-thawed blastocysts) found the same
inversion at ten times the sample size.

**Pan P et al., *Human Reproduction Open* 2025 (n=16,453).** The largest study in the field.
Transvaginal measurement at both timepoints, live birth as the primary endpoint, inverse
probability weighting with stratification on maternal age, endometrial thickness, **embryo
quality**, embryo number and developmental stage. It computes the ESI ratio verbatim. In
blastocyst transfers, women with compacted endometrium had the *lowest* hCG-positive and
clinical pregnancy rates; in cleavage-stage transfers, *expansion* predicted higher live
birth (OR 1.166, 95% CI 1.070–1.271).

**Al-Lamee H et al., *Human Reproduction Open* 2024 (21 studies, 27,857 patients).** The
most complete synthesis. Compaction raises clinical pregnancy (OR 1.83) and ongoing
pregnancy (OR 1.61) but **live birth OR 0.95 (0.87–1.04) — flat**. The PGT-A euploid
subgroup showed no clinical or ongoing pregnancy advantage at all. Their conclusion is
unambiguous: "stratification of women according to EC cannot currently be recommended in
clinical practice." Turkgeldi 2023, Chen 2023 and Feng 2024 reach the same place
independently; Feng's phrasing is that repeat measurement on transfer day "may not be
necessary or warranted."

## Where the evidence conflicts, and why

Four design differences explain almost all of the disagreement.

**1. Ultrasound route is the single biggest driver.** The positive studies from the Casper
group (Haas 2019, Zilberberg 2020) and Shah 2022 measured transvaginally at progesterone
start and **transabdominally** at transfer. Ju 2023 (positive) used transabdominal
throughout and conceded it "is less precise than vaginal ultrasound." The null studies —
Riestenberg 2021, Ye 2020, Olgan 2022, Aslih 2024, Pan 2025 — used transvaginal at both
timepoints. Chen XT's 2023 meta-analysis put a number on this: pooling only studies that
mixed transvaginal with abdominal imaging, compaction predicted ongoing pregnancy
(RR 1.69, 1.26–2.26); pooling everything, it predicted nothing (live birth RR 0.97,
0.92–1.02). **The signal tracks the measurement artefact.** Any study that measures
transvaginally at both points should expect a null.

**2. Endpoint choice manufactures apparent effects.** Compaction looks predictive of
biochemical, clinical and ongoing pregnancy, and stops looking predictive at live birth —
in every meta-analysis, and within individual studies (Akbari Sene 2026: clinical
pregnancy significant, live birth p=0.072; Jin Z 2021: clinical pregnancy significant, live
birth comparable). This pattern is what one expects from a marker correlated with early
detection or with an unmeasured confounder rather than with true endometrial receptivity.

**3. Ploidy status changes the answer.** In unscreened cohorts, endometrial variation
competes with enormous embryo-side variance; in euploid cohorts, embryo-side variance is
compressed and any true endometrial effect should be *easier* to see. It gets *smaller*
instead — pooled euploid RR 0.99 (0.86–1.13). That is the strongest single argument that
the compaction signal is not endometrial biology.

**4. Cutoffs are arbitrary and inconsistent.** Al-Lamee documented five different
definitions across 21 studies: any decrease, ≥5%, ≥10%, ≥15%, and undefined. Effects appear
and vanish depending on which is chosen, and the ≥15% stratum — where the largest effects
are reported (OPR OR 3.52 in Al-Lamee; RR 1.99 in Chen) — is also the smallest and most
selected subgroup. This is a textbook multiple-threshold problem.

**5. Endometrial thickness is not endometrial volume.** Li X 2023 measured both
prospectively in natural cycles and found thickness *increased* (11.26→11.89 mm) while
volume and blood flow *decreased* over the same interval. "Compaction" measured as a
one-dimensional thickness change may be measuring a different thing than the underlying
tissue change it is named after.

## What has NOT been done

Be blunt about this section internally. Most of what feels novel about ESI has been done.

**Already done, and repeatedly:**
- Percent change between progesterone start and transfer day — done, with the identical formula (Pan 2025, Ju 2023, Bu 2019, Jin Z 2021).
- Single blastocyst FET specifically — done (Bu 2019 n=3,091; Kaye 2021 n=232).
- Euploid-only cohorts — done four separate times (Zilberberg 2020, Riestenberg 2021, Jin Z 2021, Shah 2022) and meta-analysed (Turkgeldi 2023, 4 studies, 1,172 cycles).
- Continuous modelling of the change ratio — done (Jin Z 2021 with an adjusted OR per unit; Kaye 2021 with % change as a continuous regressor; Bu 2019 by ratio).
- Adjustment for embryo quality — done (Kaye 2021 with quantitative blastocyst morphometrics; Pan 2025 stratified/weighted; Ju 2023 by count of high-quality embryos; Gill 2023 by embryo score; Yaprak 2023 by stage).
- Live birth as primary endpoint — done at scale (Pan 2025 n=16,453; Ye 2020 n=4,465; Huang 2020 n=2,768) and in all four meta-analyses.
- Obstetric sequelae — done (Gill 2023: no association with preterm birth or placental complications).

**Genuinely not yet done:**
1. **No randomised controlled trial exists.** All four meta-analyses state this explicitly, and Al-Lamee 2024 calls for one. ESI, as a retrospective cohort, does not fill this gap.
2. **Implantation rate as a distinct, pre-specified primary endpoint is rare.** Almost the entire literature reports biochemical/clinical/ongoing pregnancy or live birth. The only retrieved study using implantation directly is Milewski 2025 — and at n=61 with an unvalidated shape index, it is not credible evidence. A properly powered implantation analysis is a defensible, if narrow, gap.
3. **No study combines all four rigour criteria simultaneously:** euploid-only, single blastocyst, transvaginal-at-both-timepoints, continuous modelling, *and* adjustment for graded blastocyst morphology, with adequate power. Kaye 2021 comes closest (single blastocyst, transvaginal, continuous, morphometric adjustment) but is mixed-ploidy and n=232 with only 19 compaction cases. Jin Z 2021 is euploid, single blastocyst, transvaginal and continuous but n=219, natural-cycle only, PGT-SR/PGT-M rather than PGT-A, and adjustment for blastocyst grade is unconfirmed.
4. **The prespecified prospective euploid modified-natural-cycle study (De la Torre Perez 2023 protocol, n=206, Frontiers in Endocrinology) may now have reported.** Its results were not located as of this search. **Re-check before submission** — if it has published, it directly occupies part of the intended space. Likewise ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04330066, "Impact of Endometrial Compaction in Euploid Frozen Embryo Transfers", whose status was not verified.

## Implications for the ESI study

**The novelty claim as stated is not defensible.** "Whether endometrial change after
progesterone predicts implantation" is not an open question in the sense of being
unexamined; it is an open question in the sense of being *contested*, with the weight of
evidence — and every meta-analysis — currently sitting on the null. The Introduction must
be written to that reality.

**What the team must NOT claim:**
- That dynamic endometrial change has not been studied, or is under-studied.
- That the ESI metric is a new index. It is endometrial compaction, expressed as a percentage change ratio, and it has been published under that formula.
- That no one has looked at euploid single blastocyst transfers. Four studies have.
- That no one has modelled the change continuously or adjusted for embryo quality. Several have.
- That a positive result would establish clinical utility. Four meta-analyses have already concluded that stratifying patients on compaction cannot be recommended, and one large prospective study warns it risks unnecessary cycle cancellation.
- Any framing that implies the field is unsettled *in favour* of compaction. The pooled live-birth estimate is flat, and the two largest single-blastocyst cohorts (Bu 2019, Pan 2025) point the *other* way.

**What the team CAN legitimately claim, if the design supports it:**
- A pre-specified **implantation** endpoint, which the field has largely skipped in favour of clinical pregnancy and live birth.
- **Continuous modelling with a pre-registered functional form**, avoiding the five-competing-cutoffs problem that Al-Lamee 2024 identified as the field's central methodological weakness.
- **Transvaginal measurement at both timepoints**, which — given Chen XT's subgroup analysis — is the design feature most likely to separate real biology from acoustic artefact.
- **Explicit adjustment for graded blastocyst morphology within a euploid-only cohort**, which isolates the endometrial contribution better than any single published study has.
- Framing the study as an **adequately powered replication/refutation** in the population where the effect should be easiest to detect and has been least reliably measured. This is honest, useful, and publishable — and it is a stronger position than an untrue priority claim.

**Two design warnings.** First, powering: Riestenberg found only 43 compaction cycles in
259, and Kaye only 19 in 232. If compaction prevalence in the ESI cohort resembles those
(the pooled estimate is 32%, but 17% in Riestenberg's transvaginal cohort), a continuous
model is essential — a dichotomised analysis will be underpowered. Second, direction: the
team should pre-specify a two-sided hypothesis. Bu 2019, Jin Z 2021, Hou 2025 and Pan 2025
all found *expansion* favourable. A one-sided compaction hypothesis is not supported by the
current evidence base.
