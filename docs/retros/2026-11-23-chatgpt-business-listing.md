## In plain words

An Instagram reel said that ChatGPT now lets a business claim a profile, much like a Google Business
listing. That is true. OpenAI launched it on November 10, 2026. For Dr. Mekhala, three things block a quick
trial:

1. Indian clinics can probably not claim a profile yet. OpenAI says local businesses can claim only US
   listings for now. Not yet confirmed for a clinic, because the claim page needs a sign-in.
2. A published profile cannot be taken down. OpenAI Support cannot delete, unpublish or hide it. The
   profile publishes itself when the identity check approves it.
3. The listing belongs to Indira IVF, not to her. Proof of ownership uses the business phone, email or
   website, which the chain controls. The NMC rules also bar a doctor from promoting her employer in her
   own name.

ChatGPT today knows her well when asked by name. It leaves out her and Indira IVF when a patient asks for
"an IVF specialist in Rajajinagar." That gap is what a listing would fix, and the fix belongs to the chain.

## What OpenAI's own pages say

Checked directly on November 23, 2026, unless marked otherwise.

- **Name and launch:** "Businesses in ChatGPT" (beta), November 10, 2026. Announcement at
  `openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/`, read by a research agent, not directly.
- **Countries:** the help article says businesses can claim "where ChatGPT is available," that availability
  "depends on the business's location and type," and that local stores and restaurants "can currently claim
  only an existing U.S. listing." India is not named. Whether a clinic counts as a local store is not said.
- **Removal:** "Only unpublished draft profiles can be deleted." Support "is unable to delete, unpublish, or
  hide a published profile."
- **Ownership checks:** business phone, business email, a DNS TXT record on the website, or Shopify.
- **Identity check:** Persona. It may ask for a government ID, business registration and proof of authority
  to represent the business.
- **Health rules for the business agent** (Business Agents Policy): it must not give advice that legally
  needs a professional license, claim credentials or guarantee results. It must not ask for health details.
  It must not downplay a possible medical emergency. OpenAI may "defer a category until we can support it
  safely." Doctors and clinics are not named as allowed or excluded.
- **Business agent availability:** US ecommerce and online service businesses only, per the help center.
  The research agent read this; it was not re-read directly.
- **Price:** not stated by OpenAI. One third-party report (VentureBeat, citing Digiday) says free at launch.
- **Not read:** `chatgpt.com/business-hub`, because it needs a sign-in. The 300-character description limit
  seen in the reel is unverified.

Sources: `help.openai.com/en/articles/20001635-setting-up-your-business-on-chatgpt` and
`openai.com/policies/business-agents-policy/`, both read directly. `openai.com/business/businesses-in-chatgpt/`
and the VentureBeat article were read only by the research agent. Do not confuse any of these with the
separate ChatGPT Ads rules (`help.openai.com/en/articles/20001534`), which restrict health advertising.

## NMC rules that apply

From the checklist built on October 19, 2026 for the audit of her LinkedIn posts. That checklist summarizes
NMC public notice R-13014/01/2024-Ethics of October 6, 2026. Re-read the notice itself before anything is
published. Both files are in `portfolio-commons/private/nmc-audit-2026-10-19/`, which git ignores.

- Show name, qualifications and registration number (3.2 Explanation III).
- No superlatives, success rates or patient counts (8.1(iii), 8.1(ix)).
- No soliciting patients: no "book now" or "DM me" (8.1(ii), 5.1).
- No testimonials or reviews (3.2 Explanation V, 8.1(xi)).
- AI-generated promotional content carries an AI mark (7.2, 7.2(c)).
- No promoting the employer hospital in her own name (8.2(iii)).
